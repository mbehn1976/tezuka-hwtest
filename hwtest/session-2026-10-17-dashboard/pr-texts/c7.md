## Spectrum peak markers and a dB scale fix (commits a8a2b8f, 0c03b8b)

**Peak markers on the Spectrum page.** The two strongest bins of the visible window get numbered triangles and a readout `P1`/`P2` (frequency in MHz, level in dB). The second peak is at least 6 bins from the first, the 3 bins around DC/LO are skipped. A `PEAK ON/OFF` field in the header switches them.

**dB scale fix.** The `/waterfall` bins are power, but the Spectrum, Radio Astronomy and Signal Classifier pages converted them with `20*log10`, so every level difference was shown twice as large. They now use `10*log10`. Default REF is 100 (was 130) and the REF unit reads dB, not dBm (the level is uncalibrated).

Evidence on hardware: Pluto sends two tones with different amplitude at 10 MS/s (2400.771 and 2400.791 MHz), the Pluto+ receives, page host-served.

| | Python decode of /waterfall | Spectrum page | Complex I/Q stream |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | bin 2364, 2400.771484 MHz, 10*log10(2.21e9) = 93.4 dB | 2400.771861 MHz, 93.4 dB | 66.2 dB |
| P2 | bin 2372, 2400.791016 MHz, 10*log10(1.25e8) = 81.0 dB | 2400.791402 MHz, 81.1 dB | 53.8 dB |
| P1 - P2 | 12.4 dB | 12.3 dB (was 24.6 dB) | 12.4 dB |

The two tones differ by 12 dB in amplitude (4x, 16x in power), and all three sources now agree. Radio Astronomy and the Classifier read the same peak with the same signal (102.4 and 102.5 dB at 2.5 MS/s). The Radio Astronomy auto scale outlier filter was adjusted to the new clamp (-100 dB instead of -200 dB).

Not changed: the page maps bin i to i/(n-1) after dropping the step-index value f[0], which puts the frequency about 0.4 kHz above the decode at 10 MS/s (below one bin of 2.4 kHz; the cursor and axes use the same mapping). The old 20*log10 may have been a deliberate scaling choice, please tell me if you prefer the old scale.

Screenshots and the README section "Spectrum page: peak markers and dB scale" are in `Dashboard/docs/complex-iq/`. Firmware images with this change are being built and will be tested on both boards before the next update.

## Correction to my FSW explanation, and the weak-line test (commit 7d61b91)

In my previous comment (https://github.com/F5OEO/tezuka_fw/pull/500#issuecomment-6235998399) I explained the small SIG - REF values of a strong CW tone in Radio Astronomy FSW mode with a slow MQTT retune. I have since measured it and that explanation was wrong.

**What is measured now**

* An MQTT retune command reaches the LO within 130 to 220 ms (six retunes timed on the Pluto+, polled in sysfs on the board).
* The /waterfall frames lag behind: after each retune the first frame that arrives still shows the old LO position. At a 0.42 s frame period the first frame after the command was stale in 4 of 6 retunes and every later frame was new.
* At the page's 1 fps this means about one stale frame in every dwell, i.e. 1/INTEG of the window (20 % at INTEG 5 s, about 3 % at 30 s). A stale fraction p of a tone that is 70 dB above the noise limits the SIG - REF ratio to about 10*log10(1/p): about 7 dB at INTEG 5 s (I saw +8 dB), about 15 dB at INTEG 30 s.

**Weak-line test** (Pluto+ page, INTEG 30 s, OFS 500 kHz, Pluto TX gain -64 dB so the line is about 10 dB above the local noise, 39.3 dB in the plain spectrum): after both dwells (about 75 s) SIG - REF shows the line at 2400.771861 MHz at +16.2 dB (marker P1), a flat baseline close to 0 dB (the bandpass shape cancels), and the mirror image of the line 0.5 MHz lower as a negative spike (about -12 dB at 2400.27 MHz), as frequency switching should do. The second marker (+9.6 dB at 2399.9756 MHz) is one of the transmitter's spurs.

So FSW works for a weak line. For a strong line the stale frame limits the ratio. Skipping the first frame of every dwell would remove that limit; I have not implemented it because it changes the page's behaviour (it is not part of this PR's scope unless you want it).

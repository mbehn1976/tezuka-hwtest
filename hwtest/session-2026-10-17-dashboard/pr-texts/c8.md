## Firmware images with the Spectrum markers and the 10*log10 fix, tested on both boards (commit 56bbe7a)

Pluto flashed from the .frm, Pluto+ from the SD image. Both boards serve the new page from the image (the /root/index.html md5 equals the build output) and the bitstreams in the images match their packages.

**RF suite** (I/Q check with automatic carrier offset, phase check, frame phase check at 2.5, 10, 20, 30.72 and 61.44 MS/s on each board): 30 steps, 69 checks ok, 1 FAIL, every settings restore matched.

* Frame rotation measured minus predicted: -0.0010 to +0.0024 rad, scatter at most 0.0018 rad.
* OFDM log correlation 0.989 to 0.999; phase derived frequency minus time domain frequency -12.9 to +4.8 Hz; model error at most 1.16 deg and 0.22 dB.
* The FAIL: Pluto+ at 2.5 MS/s, tone at -600 kHz, raw capture against magnitude waterfall correlation 0.961, with the per-frame I/Q peak on bins 984 and 985 (magnitude peak 984).
* Re-run of that check alone, 14 times: 13 ok, 1 FAIL. The second FAIL had correlation 1.0 but the per-frame I/Q peak split over two bins (985 and 986). The check requires one bin. The carrier offset between the boards drifts (-48.4 kHz here), which puts this tone at bin position 985.59 to 985.68, close to the half-bin boundary where the peak of a single frame can fall on either neighbour. That explains the split peak. I did not prove it explains the 0.961 correlation of the first FAIL. The firmware reads the right bins in all cases (raw, magnitude and I/Q peaks agree within one bin), so I count this as a test criterion that is too strict near a half-bin position, not a data error. I can relax it to accept the neighbouring bin if you want.
